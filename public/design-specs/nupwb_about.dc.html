Akụkọ Anyị
Our Story
Nwoke Udi began with one tapper, one gourd and a bench under a zinc roof on Ogui Road. People stopped for a cup on the way home and stayed until the generators went off.
The bench became a bar, and the bar became a kitchen. The palm still arrives the same way it always has — carried in before noon from the groves outside Enugu, poured the same afternoon, never kept overnight. Akulu for the ones who want it strong. Soft pammy for everyone else.
What we serve beside it has not changed much either. Isi ewu the way our mothers cut it, nkwobi in palm oil that tastes of the pot it was made in, abacha sixteen different ways. Come hungry, come early, and do not plan anything for afterwards.
What we're known for
Fresh Palm Wine
Tapped in the morning, on the table by afternoon. Akulu and soft pammy, by the gourd or the can.
Traditional Igbo Kitchen
Isi ewu, nkwobi, ugba and abacha, cooked to order. Nothing sits under a lamp waiting for you.
Grills & Shisha
Barbecue fish and whole roasted chicken off the coals, and shisha at the back tables after dark.
